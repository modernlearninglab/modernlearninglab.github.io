How I run a project

My approach

Milestones give clients and stakeholders predictable checkpoints. Sprints inside development give the creative team room to iterate without renegotiating the schedule every week. Backlog Maintenance is added only when a program keeps living after launch.

Project portfolio

01

Train-the-trainer redesign and LMS launch

Wealthy Habits, a nonprofit financial literacy organization  |  Contract engagement

Every new cohort of trainers waited on the founder to deliver a week of live training. That limited how fast the organization could grow and left partner requests unanswered.

What I did

Ran a structured discovery phase against the existing lesson inventory, built a prototype to confirm scope before production began, and converted a 35 hour live curriculum into modular self-paced lessons with story-driven narratives written for a youth audience.

How I managed it

Ten milestones with defined acceptance criteria, staged delivery in batches of 25, 50, 70, and 80 lessons, and review checkpoints for instructional quality, content sign-off, and final visual design.

Team coordination

Directed volunteer student designers on first-draft production, routed polish work to a remote senior designer, and sent instructional review to a facilitation expert. Wrote the checklists and templates that kept output consistent across contributors with different skill levels.

Result

A self-paced program the founder no longer has to deliver personally, standardized materials with quizzes and mock-session assessments, and modular access so partners can pull individual topics without completing the full path.

Role
Project Manager and Lead Learning Experience Designer
Scope
80 lessons rebuilt into a self-paced train-the-trainer program with two learner paths
Timeline
December 2025 to March 2026, 10 milestones
Team
Client founder, instructional reviewer, final designer, and a rotating bench of student volunteer designers
Stack
LearnDash LMS, Canva, Google Vids, AI-assisted drafting
Why it transfers

Client-services delivery with a single decision maker, a creative production pipeline, story-driven content, hand-offs across Discovery, Design, Development, QA, and Delivery, and an LMS deployment at the end.

02

Client advisory services CPE program

Association of International Certified Professional Accountants  |  Learning Product Development

Leadership set a fixed launch date and asked for 40 courses. The production math did not support it.

What I did

Reframed the request as a minimum viable cohort of 15 fully reviewed and compliant courses at launch, with the remaining courses sequenced into follow-on releases. Supported the recommendation with production capacity, review cycle length, and compliance turnaround figures.

How I managed it

Alpha reviews focused on substance and structure, SME scheduling built around the QA bottleneck, and a risk register tracking the dependencies most likely to slip, primarily SME availability and compliance review capacity.

Result

A defensible launch scope, a stakeholder group aligned on what ships and when, and a release sequence that held the quality standard.

Role
Project Manager, Learning
Scope
40 course continuing professional education program for the client advisory services practice area
Limits
NASBA QAS compliance, verified learning objectives, seat time, and assessment rigor
Team
Learning experience designers, subject matter experts, content reviewers, compliance, production
Why it transfers

Managing expectations, negotiating priorities, and escalating early under a tight timeline, with quality standards enforced at every stage of production.

03

Project management platform migration

Association of International Certified Professional Accountants  |  Learning Product Development

The team's working process had grown up around the old tool and was largely undocumented. Recovering it was the bulk of the work.

What I did

Audited existing boards and in-flight work, mapped the delivery workflow the team was using in practice, and rebuilt project structures, statuses, and request intake in the new platform to match it.

How I managed it

Phased pilot with a defined cohort, adoption support and documentation built alongside the configuration, and success criteria agreed before migration began.

Result

A board structure the team can run projects in without workarounds, and a repeatable pattern for onboarding new project types.

Role
Project Manager and process lead
Scope
Migration of the learning product development team from Workfront to Jira and Confluence
Approach
90 day phased pilot ahead of full rollout
Team
Project managers, learning experience designers, production staff, platform administrators
Why it transfers

Taking over projects already in progress and rebuilding the boards running them, including request intake and status structures the team could use without workarounds.

04

Property sales AI product launch

Left Main REI  |  Product and Knowledge Manager

An AI feature inside a real estate CRM that matched county property sales records against open leads and closed the ones already sold.

What I did

Led user research, built user journey maps and wireframe prototypes in Figma, wrote product requirements, defined success metrics, facilitated weekly design reviews and stakeholder alignment, and owned beta customer onboarding.

Learning and knowledge assets

Produced job aids, knowledge articles, and a consolidated desk reference that served as the single source of truth for customers, support, and sales.

Risk handling

Late in development we found no guardrail for reversing records the algorithm had closed in error. I worked with engineering on scenario planning and shipped a fix inside the existing deadline.
